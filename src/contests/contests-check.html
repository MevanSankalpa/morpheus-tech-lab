The Contests mode (#77)

Runs on its own when the page opens, in the frame below; takes about 40 seconds, two of them Speed runs. Keep this tab in front.

Running…

    By hand, in the lab itself

    • Settings → Contests → Apply: the panel sits in the middle, clear of the HUD and the control bar, and reads well.
    • Speed's page: Run stands out as the thing to press.
    • During a run, nothing is drawn behind the panel, the clock's arc empties smoothly over the 6 seconds, and the chart climbs and rescales its generations axis as the run passes each step.
    • Hovering the chart shows a crosshair and the time and generations of the nearest point; its labels never overlap or run off the edge, at full width and at phone width.
    • The results card looks good in a screenshot: the score leads, the chart follows, the rest is secondary.
    • Export result: Chrome and Edge ask where to save speed-1-N.json (speed-1-1000-Tms.json at the ceiling); Firefox and Safari download it. node scripts/verify.mjs speed-1-N.json says verified, with the score.
    • The results card's browser line is right in Chrome, Edge, Firefox and Safari.
    • At phone width (devtools), the panel sits above the control bar and scrolls if it must.
    • The GPU toggle's popover says contests run on the CPU.
    • Watch: the run plays back in 3D as in Explore, the camera follows the pattern as it grows, and past the render budget the chunks show as boxes. Dragging the scrub bar back and forth feels responsive, and the view settles on the generation let go at.
    • Watch's bar sits above the control bar, clear of it and of the HUD, at full width and at phone width.
    • Submit by email, in Chrome, Firefox and Safari with a mail app set up: the file downloads and a new email opens to lab@morpheustech.net, with the subject and the text filled in, line breaks kept.
    • The Copy buttons beside the address, the subject and the text each put it on the clipboard; pasted into a webmail compose window, the text keeps its lines.
    • The Submit section and the Hall of Fame (staged by the check above, or with real entries) read well at full width and at phone width: long names end in an ellipsis, and Watch stays on the row.
